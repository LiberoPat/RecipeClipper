Green Bean Casserole with Crispy Onions

  • Servings: 6, but more if you have a lot of other sides
  • Time: 1 hour
  • Source: Adapted a little from Alton Brown and trial-and-error
  • Print

I gently updated this recipe in 2026 with oven-optional directions and more detailed do-ahead and reheating instructions.

    Crispy Onions
  • 2 medium yellow onions, halved and thinly sliced
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons panko or plain breadcrumbs
  • 1/2 teaspoon table salt
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Canola, safflower, peanut or other high-heat oil, for deep-frying
    • Mushroom Sauce
  • 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 12 ounces mushrooms, thinly sliced or coarsely chopped
  • Few gratings fresh nutmeg (optional)
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • 3/4 teaspoon table salt
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 cups vegetable or chicken broth
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
    • Assembly
  • 1 to 1 1/2 pounds green beans, trimmed and halved (see note; I prefer more)

Make the crispy onions: Toss onion with flour, breadcrumbs, salt and pepper. Heat a 1/2-inch or so of oil in a 12-inch cast iron skillet (or, in your favorite stove-to-oven braiser) until a drop of water flicked into it will hiss and sputter. Add onions, just a handful at a time in something close to a single layer, and fry until a light golden brown. Remove with a spider or large slotted spoon, shake off the excess oil, back into the skillet, then spread onions out on paper towels to drain. I like to finish them with a little more salt. Repeat with remaining onions. Set aside until needed; this makes a lot but we never mind the extra. [If you’d like to bake the onions instead, the instructions are at the end.]

Heat oven: To 400°F. [But read the Note at the end about skipping the oven, if needed.]

Prepare the beans: Bring a pot of salted water to boil and boil green beans for 2 to 5 minutes (2 is for beans that stay semi-crisp; 5 is for fully-cooked, tender beans). Drain beans, then plunge them into ice water to preserve their color and crisp. Drain again, pat them dry with a towel, and set aside. (If you are adamant about only using one pot, you can boil them in your the same pan you used for the other steps.)

Make the mushroom sauce: Over medium-high heat, melt butter in the bottom of a heavy pan. Add the mushrooms, salt, and pepper and sauté them until they release their liquid, 3 to 5 minutes. Add the garlic and cook one minute more. Add the flour and stir it until it fully coats the mushrooms. Add the broth, 1/4 cup at a time, stirring the whole time. Simmer mixture for 1 minute, then add cream and bring back to a simmer, cooking until the sauce thickens a bit, about 5 to 6 minutes, stirring frequently.

Assemble and bake: Add cooked greens beans to sauce and stir until they are coated. Transfer to the oven and bake for 12 to 15 minutes, or until sauce is bubbling and the green beans are tender. Briefly remove from the oven, sprinkle the fried onions on top, and return the dish to the oven for 2 minutes. [This step is only to rewarm the onions if they were cold; I often skip this if they’re still lukewarm from frying.]

Serve: Right away.

Do ahead, a few ways: If you’re making this to serve at a later time, you can pause at a couple places. Green beans can be cooked and kept plain in fridge until needed, at least two days. Green beans can also be combined with mushroom sauce and kept refrigerated for up to two days. Onions can be made long in advance (up to 2-3 days). If not using them that day, you can store them in a container in the fridge. Keep the onions off the casserole and completely separate until right before serving.

To rewarm: Reheat the green beans and sauce in a covered dish until hot. Add chilled onions only for the last 5 minutes; add room temperature onions for the last 2 minutes. [Reheating chilled green beans/sauce takes much longer than cooking them while already warm and if the onions are on the whole time, they will burn.]

A few more notes:

  • While there is a range of suggested green bean weights, I always use the higher one of 1 1/2 pounds for a more generous and less creamy casserole.
  • Every time I make this, I question whether the baking step is absolutely necessary. It’s not. You can simply cook the beans in the mushroom sauce at a low simmer on the stove until they’re as soft as you prefer — I often find that 5 to 10 minutes is plenty, stirring it a few times. If your pan has a lid, put it on for more even cooking. This is great to keep in mind if oven space is at a premium that day. I also find that if the fried onions aren’t fully reheated with the green beans, it’s not the end of the world. The warmth of the green beans and mushroom sauce usually heats them sufficiently.
  • If you’d like to bake the onions instead of frying them, spread them on one big parchment-lined baking sheet at 425°F for about 30 minutes, tossing them around a few times to ensure even cooking, and keeping an eye on them. However, I found this pesky — it’s much harder to get them evenly crisp and golden and they’re prone to getting too dark before being fully cooked. I always end up deep-frying them and having zero regrets. It’s Thanksgiving. Let’s do this properly.
